# All 40 questions and answer options

This is the exact publishable, standardized question bank used in `index.html`. Questions 1–39 are multiple choice (up to three choices), and Question 40 is an open reflection with optional self-selected themes. The original first-person version of Question 15 appears in the appendix below, because the two live interviews used different questions at that position.

## Question 1

- **A.** I immediately start figuring out what needs to happen. The uncertainty bothers me less once I have a plan.
- **B.** I become very aware of everyone else's emotional state and start tracking who is frightened, upset, withdrawing, or likely to cause a problem.
- **C.** I feel irritated that nobody else seems to be seeing what feels obvious to me, though I may not show it.
- **D.** I instinctively hang back for a moment. I want to understand the whole situation before I influence what happens.
- **E.** I feel a surge of responsibility. Even if I never agreed to be responsible, it now feels difficult not to take care of the situation.
- **F.** Something else.

## Question 2

- **D.** I assume they'll come to me when they're ready and mostly go on with my day.
- **E.** I start mentally reviewing what I might have done wrong, even before I know whether I actually did anything.
- **F.** I withdraw too. If they are shutting me out, I'm unlikely to keep reaching.
- **G.** Something else.

## Question 3

- **D.** I replay the situation repeatedly, trying to understand exactly where I went wrong.
- **E.** I can own the mistake fairly directly, but I need some distance before I can talk about it well.
- **F.** I become unusually accommodating afterward, almost trying to compensate for having let them down.

## Question 4

## Question 7

- **B.** I initially minimize my anger or try to understand their side, sometimes to the point that I lose track of what I actually feel.
- **C.** I become colder and more distant. I need the other person to recognize that something has changed before I can soften.
- **D.** I can become intensely expressive—sharp, emotional, or forceful—and then often feel guilty about how strongly I reacted.
- **E.** I am more comfortable being angry on someone else’s behalf than on my own. When I am the one who was hurt, anger often turns into sadness, anxiety, or self-questioning.
- **F.** I hold it in until I am certain I am entitled to be angry. By that point, I may have accumulated much more resentment than the immediate situation warrants.
